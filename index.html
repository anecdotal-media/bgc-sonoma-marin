How we work

The stories are already happening. The skill is spotting them.

Great content doesn’t start with a camera. It starts with noticing. This is the method from the playbook we built for Boys & Girls Clubs of the Permian Basin, and it’s what we’d teach your team.

Signal 1

A first

First basket, first day, first win. Firsts are always emotional. Think of the first kids through the doors of a new Clubhouse.

Signal 2

A surprise

A kid doing something you didn’t expect, or staff stepping outside their comfort zone. The unexpected is watchable.

Signal 3

A contrast

Something that challenges assumptions: a young kid explaining code, a shy kid giving a speech.

Signal 4

A real relationship

A kid and a staff member with visible trust and warmth. Connection on camera stops people mid-scroll.

The one-question test

“Would I stop scrolling for this?”

If the answer is no, it isn’t a story yet. That includes anything that sounds like a press release: “Today we hosted our annual…” Nobody watches that.

Then five shots on a phone

  1. The hook shot. The most emotional moment, filmed close. It goes first.
  2. The wide shot. Where we are: the gym, the classroom, the field.
  3. The detail shot. Hands dribbling, a pencil writing, a face concentrating.
  4. The reaction shot. A coach, a parent, a teammate. Reactions tell viewers how to feel.
  5. The closing shot. A handshake, kids walking away, a final laugh.
